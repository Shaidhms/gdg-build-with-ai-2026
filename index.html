Session 1 · Android & AI

Stop Building AI Demos.
Start Building AI Products.

Most AI prototypes never make it to production. Somasundaram Mahesh, a senior Android engineer at Disney+ Hotstar with 10+ years of experience, breaks down the exact reasons why — and what it actually takes to ship an AI-powered product that users trust and return to.

SM
Somasundaram Mahesh
Senior Android Engineer · Disney+ Hotstar · Kotlin Multiplatform
msomu.com
Key Insights
01
The Demo Trap
80% of AI POCs never reach production. Demos only cover the happy path — no edge cases, no latency thinking, no returning users.
02
Context Window Is Your Whole Codebase
Agentic tools (Cursor, Claude Code) send your entire project as context — every exchange grows the token count until the window fills.
03
AI Is Non-Deterministic
Same input, different output every time. You cannot test AI like traditional code — you need evaluation datasets with expected outputs and scoring.
04
The 4 Pillars: Demo vs Product
Reliability, Consistency, User Experience (streaming, progressive UI), and Cost (caching, right-sizing the model).
05
The 80 / 20 Reality
80% of an AI product is now trivially buildable. The last 20% — reliability, trust, graceful failure — is what earns returning users.
06
Always Have a Fallback
Validate every AI output before showing it. Set rate-limit guards, log everything, and alert on unexpected cost or behavior changes.
Before any AI response reaches a user:

→ Handle rate limits and errors gracefully — always have a fallback path
→ Validate every AI output before displaying it to the user
→ Stream responses — never make users wait for a full completion
→ Log every input and output with latency, cost, and failure tracking
→ Cache aggressively — at 1K users, $30/day compounds quickly
→ Set alerts for unexpected changes in AI behavior or cost spikes
Type 1 — Traditional: Opens IDE, writes code manually, searches documentation.

Type 2 — AI-Assisted: Copies code into ChatGPT, pastes results back. IDE remains the base.

Type 3 — AI-Native: Uses Claude/Cursor inside the IDE. AI has full code context. No copy-paste.

Type 4 — Agentic Engineer: Assigns tasks to Claude Code or Codex. AI creates branches, writes code, opens PRs. Human reviews and merges.
→ During IPL, traffic spikes are predictable — plan for them, not against them
→ Background servers shift dynamically to prioritize cricket streaming
→ Intentional degradation: some features are deliberately throttled under peak load
→ Authentication flows are simplified when 100M users log in simultaneously
→ Key principle: design for graceful degradation, not just peak success

"Don't stop your AI demos — convert them into actual AI products. The demo is where you learn. The product is where you earn trust."

— Somasundaram Mahesh, GDG Chennai Build with AI 2026

Session 2 · SLMs & Fine-Tuning

Small Language Models,
Big Impact

Not every AI problem needs a frontier model. Sanju Sivalingam — head of design at Poly and founder of a design studio — takes a practical look at small language models: why they matter, when they outperform large models, and how to fine-tune one with surprisingly little data.

SS
Sanju Sivalingam
Head of Design, Poly · Founder, Mr. US Design Studio · Kovan Labs
sanju.sh
Key Insights
01
4 Reasons to Choose SLMs
Cost (run far cheaper), Privacy (data stays on your infrastructure), Speed (faster on-device inference), Customization (fine-tune for your domain).
02
On-Device AI Is Here
SLMs run on phones and edge hardware — Pixel, Samsung, Apple. Google and Apple are embedding AI inference directly at the OS level.
03
Function Calling Demystified
Models don't just generate text — they call APIs. You define tools; the model decides which to call, executes it, and returns structured data.
04
Fine-Tune Decision Framework
System prompts solve 90%. RAG handles knowledge-heavy tasks. Fine-tune only for consistent style, format, or deep domain expertise.
05
Less Data Than You Think
~1,000 curated examples for style fine-tuning. ~400 for format tasks. Quality beats quantity every time.
06
T-Shaped vs Broad Knowledge
Frontier models are generalists. A fine-tuned SLM is a deep expert in one domain — and often outperforms the big model on that specific task.
The Four SLM Families Worth Knowing
Phi-4
3.8B parameters · Microsoft
Reasoning King
Punches far above its weight in math and structured reasoning despite its small parameter count.
Gemma 3
2B – 27B · Google DeepMind
Multilingual
Strong multilingual support, runs on-device, best-in-class for Google ecosystem integration.
Qwen 3
0.5B – 235B · Alibaba
Dark Horse
Massive size range. Regularly outperforms models twice its size in coding benchmarks.
Llama 3.3
1B – 8B · Meta
Best Ecosystem
Largest open-source community, most tooling and deployment options. The safe, proven default.

Session 3 · MCP & Web

WebMCP: Deep Dive into
MCP on the Other Side of the Screen

Varun Raja Manoharan — Google Developer Expert for Firebase, CTO of GbScript — demonstrated WebMCP: bringing the Model Context Protocol to browser environments, letting web developers wire AI models to tools and APIs without complex backend infrastructure.

VR
Varun Raja Manoharan
Google Developer Expert (Firebase) · CTO & Co-Founder, GbScript
varunraj.in
Key Insights
01
MCP Comes to the Browser
Model Context Protocol has been CLI and backend-only. WebMCP brings it to web environments — browser apps can now natively connect AI models to tools and live APIs.
02
Built for Developers
WebMCP is a developer-first tool — enabling tighter integration between web UI, AI reasoning, and backend services without rebuilding each layer from scratch.
03
Firebase as the AI Backend
Combining Firebase's real-time infrastructure with AI workflows creates reactive AI applications. Varun demonstrated this integration as a GDE Firebase specialist.
04
Practical & Code-Forward
20 slides packed with practical patterns — designed for developers who want to wire AI into their web stack immediately, not in theory.

Event Summary

What the Day Was Really About

Three sessions, one consistent thread: the gap between knowing AI and shipping AI.

01
Ship, Don't Demo
Stop optimizing for impressive demos. Think in production-grade systems from day one — edge cases, returning users, failure modes.
02
Right Model for the Job
A fine-tuned 3.8B SLM on your domain data can outperform GPT-4 on your specific task — at a fraction of the cost and latency.
03
Observe Everything
Log every input and output. Track latency, cost, failure rates. AI failures are often silent and subtle — you won't know unless you watch.
04
Stream First
If your AI takes more than two seconds to respond, stream the output. Users tolerate slow typing. They don't tolerate blank screens.
05
Trust Is the Product
The last 20% isn't features — it's trust. Consistency, graceful failure, and predictable behavior are what bring users back.
06
MCP Is Going Mainstream
From CLI agents to browsers, the Model Context Protocol is becoming the standard wiring layer between AI models and the real world.
